# Woodshop buy list, build-out and running costs

How to turn a tool package (see `woodshop-tools.md`) into machines in a working shop. The listings below are a dated snapshot for the Greenpoint, Brooklyn plan; they go stale within days, so re-run the search before quoting any of them.

## How to find live used listings

- eBay is readable from here. Search local pickup only, used, within 100 miles of the shop's ZIP: `https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw=<query>&LH_ItemCondition=3000&LH_LPickup=1&_sadis=100&_stpos=<zip>`.
- The distance shown in eBay search results is unreliable. Open each item page and read the pickup town from its map link before listing it.
- Take prices from the search result row, not the item page, which mixes in prices of other items.
- Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace can't be scraped; give the owner saved-search links instead.
- GovDeals search works, but its state filter doesn't apply to scraped pages; say how many lots were nationwide.
- For each listing record: link, asking price (and OBO or auction), pickup town, voltage and phase, and anything the seller says about condition. Flag conflicts between the title and the description.

## Build-out, step by step

1. **Before signing (week 0).** Get GMDC's answers in writing on power, venting, finishing, loading and hours. Ask for the unit's floor plan, and redraw the layout to it. Price general liability insurance; most leases want about $1M, with the landlord named as additional insured.
2. **Power (weeks 1-2).** A licensed electrician adds 240 V circuits: about 30 A for the table saw, 30 A for the jointer-planer, 20-30 A each for the cyclone and the 21" bandsaw, and 120 V outlets every 6 ft along the bench wall. Run the machines off wall or ceiling drops so no cords cross the floor. If the building only has three-phase power, buy three-phase machines and skip the converters.
3. **Dust (week 2).** Put the cyclone near the milling machines, with the shortest runs to the jointer-planer and the saw. Use a 6" main duct and 4-5" drops with a blast gate at each machine, all in grounded metal duct. Use the Festool extractor for the Domino, sanders and track saw.
4. **Move machines in (weeks 2-3).** Group pickups by area (the two Lakewood machines in one trip, for example). Rent a box truck with a liftgate, and bring a pallet jack, a furniture dolly, straps and 2x4 skids. Book the freight elevator. Hire a rigger for anything over about 600 lb or up stairs. The kei trucks can't carry machines.
5. **Set up (week 3).** Level the machines, align the saw's fence and blade, set the jointer tables, put new blades and knives in everything, and test the cyclone at each gate.
6. **Benches and storage (weeks 3-4).** Build the two workbenches first, using the jointer-planer and saw. Then make the lumber rack, clamp rack, hand-tool wall and finishing corner.
7. **Safety (before the first cut).** Fire extinguishers by the door and in the finishing corner, an oily-rag can, a first-aid kit, push sticks and featherboards, and eye, ear and dust protection. No smoking anywhere in the shop.
8. **Workflow.** Lumber comes in at the door and goes to the rack, then through milling (jointer-planer, bandsaw), then sizing (table saw), joinery at the benches, assembly, and finishing. Each step moves away from the door, so wood never doubles back.

## Running the shop

These are my estimates for a one-person shop, not quotes.

**Monthly costs:** rent about $3,200-5,600 until GMDC quotes, insurance about $180, electricity about $200-400, consumables (blades, sandpaper, glue, finish) about $300, plus lumber for each job.

**What earns money early:**
- **Bench rental.** Rent 2-3 bench spots to other makers at about $400-700 a month each, in line with Brooklyn shared shops. Three renters can cover about half the rent.
- **Dovetail classes.** A weekend class for 4-6 people at $250-400 each. It ties straight into the pop-up dovetail duels and the kei trucks.
- **Small batches.** Laser-cut joinery boxes from the xTool P3, sold online and at the pop-ups.
- **Commissions.** Furniture is the main income once the shop runs.
- **Milling service.** Flatten and thickness boards for other makers by the hour once the jointer-planer is in.

**A first year that works:**
- Months 0-1: lease, insurance and electrical. Buy the jointer-planer, cyclone and bandsaw.
- Month 2: benches built, the first bench renters move in, and the first small batch goes on sale.
- Month 3: the first dovetail class and the first pop-up with the kei trucks.
- Months 4-6: the SawStop and the rest of the tools. Two classes a month and one commission a month.
- Months 6-12: grow commissions, with classes and bench rental as the base that pays the rent.

**Break-even guess:** with rent around $4,500 a month, three bench renters (about $1,800) and two classes (about $2,400 for 8 people) nearly cover rent, insurance and power. One commission a month on top makes the shop profitable.

## Lighting (estimate)

About 50 foot-candles general and 100 at benches and the saw. For about 1,900 sq ft that is roughly 160,000 lumens of fixtures (about 32 four-foot LED fixtures at 5,000 lm), plus two over each bench. 5000 K, CRI 90+ so finishes read true.

## Day-one consumables and safety, bought new (about $2,000-2,800, estimate)

Saw blades (rip, combination, crosscut, dado), bandsaw blades (resaw and curve), spare cutter inserts, sandpaper, glue, finish (hardwax oil, shellac), respirators with P100 filters, ear and eye protection, two 10 lb ABC extinguishers, an oily-waste can, first aid, a 30-gallon flammables cabinet, push sticks and featherboards.
