# Essential woodshop tools (solid-wood furniture, 1-2 people)

A starting list for a small professional shop that builds solid-wood furniture with real joinery (hand-cut dovetails, mortise and tenon, laminations). Prices were read from retailer or maker pages on 2026-11-25 (**V**, verified) or are estimates (**E**) to be priced before buying. Prices are before sales tax and freight. Remove anything the owner already has, and re-check every price on the day you buy.

## Total

About **$31,000 to $46,000** for everything above. The largest swing items are the jointer-planer quote and the electrical work, so get those two priced first.

## Phase it if needed

1. Day one: bench, hand tools, bandsaw, table saw, extractor, clamps, safety (about $13,000-17,500).
2. Add the jointer-planer and cyclone when rough lumber volume justifies it.
3. Add the drill press, router table and Domino as projects need them.

## Notes

- Buying used (machines especially) can cut 30-50%; check the motor phase and voltage against the space.
- Ask the space about phase and amps before ordering any machine; many come in single-phase and three-phase versions.
- Keep receipts in the LLC's document folder; equipment is a business asset for the accountant.

### Buy new anyway

Saw blades, router bits, bandsaw blades, jointer and planer knives (or a spiral head), HEPA filters, sharpening stones, a dovetail saw (a new one is cheap and sharp), eye, ear and lung protection, fire extinguishers, the oily-waste can, extension cords and anything with a battery. A blade-brake cartridge for a used SawStop.

### Where to look

- Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist (NY, NJ, CT, Hudson Valley): most volume; school and retiring-hobbyist shops.
- Auctions: AJ Willner Auctions (NJ), Local Line Auctions (NY), Municibid and GovDeals (school surplus), Proxibid, AuctionZip, IRS Auctions. Add buyer's premium (often 15%) and NY sales tax (8%+), and pick-up deadlines.
- Estate sales: estatesales.net, estatesale.com (search "woodworking", "shop").
- Dealers and classifieds: woodweb.com machinery exchange; preownedwoodworkingmachinery.com (its sold archive is a price check); Sawmill Creek and Fine Woodworking classifieds; SawStop and Felder/Hammer dealers for demo and trade-in units.
- Vintage hand tools: Patrick Leach's monthly tool list, Hyperkitten, tool swaps (Mid-West Tool Collectors Association meets).

### What to check before paying

- Run it under load. Listen for bearing noise; watch for vibration.
- Motor plate: voltage and phase (three-phase needs a converter), horsepower.
- Table saw: arbor runout with a dial indicator, cracked trunnions, fence locks square, blade tilts smoothly. SawStop: brake status light normal, cartridge present, ask whether the brake has fired.
- Jointer: infeed and outfeed tables flat and coplanar (straightedge), knives or head condition, gibs tight.
- Planer: feed rollers grip, bed flat, no snipe on a test board.
- Bandsaw: tyres, wheels coplanar, guides and bearings, tension spring.
- Cast iron: cracks and welds, pitted tops (surface rust cleans off; pits don't).
- Missing guards, manuals, fences and wrenches lower the price.
- Moving: machines weigh 300-700 lb. A kei truck's payload is only about 350 kg, so heavy machines go by rigger or rental truck; ask the building about freight elevator hours. NYC riggers charge roughly $200-600 per pick (estimate).
