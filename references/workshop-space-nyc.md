# NYC workshop space reference

Public facts as read on 2026-11-25 from the landlords' and operators' own pages. Rents and openings change weekly: re-read each page before quoting it, and quote the date you read it.

## Nonprofit industrial landlord: GMDC

Greenpoint Manufacturing and Design Center (gmdconline.org) is a nonprofit industrial developer. It buys and rehabs old factories and leases small units to manufacturers and artisans. Its tenant list already includes woodworking shops.

- Openings page: https://gmdconline.org/spaces-for-rent/ . Each listing gives size and building only; no rent, power or floor details are posted. Inquiries go by email to GMDC's leasing contact named on that page.

- Rent: not posted. The only published GMDC figure found is its Brownsville Industrial Center at about $14.50 to $17.50 per sq ft per year (2026 search result; Brownsville, not Greenpoint). Greenpoint market asks for comparison: $19 to $45 per sq ft per year (236 Greenpoint Ave, 300 Kingsland Ave listings, 2026). Brooklyn industrial average ask is about $26 per sq ft per year (Cushman & Wakefield).
- Rough monthly math: rent per sq ft per year x sq ft / 12. A 1,930 sq ft unit at $20 to $35 is about $3,200 to $5,600 a month. That is an estimate until GMDC quotes.

## What to check before calling a space a fit

Ask the landlord in writing:

1. Is woodworking an allowed use under the certificate of occupancy and zoning (M districts usually are)? Any current woodshop tenants in the building?
2. Electrical: amps and voltage available to the unit, 230 V single-phase or three-phase, and who pays for new circuits.
3. Dust collection: can a cyclone be ducted, and can it vent or return filtered air? Any noise limits or hours?
4. Finishing: is oil finishing allowed; is spraying allowed (needs an FDNY permit, ventilation per the Mechanical Code and Class I Div 2 wiring)?
5. Freight elevator and loading dock size and hours; ground-floor or not; where vehicles can park or load.
6. Rent per sq ft, what it includes (electric, heat, taxes, common charges), escalations, term, renewal option, security deposit, personal guarantee.
7. Insurance the lease requires (general liability limit, additional insured wording). NY general liability for a 1-4 person business averages about $180/mo (MoneyGeek, 2026); a woodworker's $1M policy runs about $500-$1,500/yr nationally.
8. Build-out: who does and pays for electrical, lighting, sprinklers; any rent-free fit-out period.

## Rules and costs worth knowing

- FDNY bans smoking in woodworking facilities (Fire Code ch. 28). Keep oily finishing rags (oil finishes such as hardwax oil) in a closed metal oily-waste can; they can self-ignite.
- NYC Commercial Rent Tax applies only in Manhattan south of 96th Street, so a Brooklyn lease doesn't pay it.
- Official FDNY interpretations come only in writing through its Public Inquiry Form, not by phone.
